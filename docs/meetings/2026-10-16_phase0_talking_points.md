# Phase 0 — talking points for a2o (meeting 2026-10-16)

## The one-sentence case

The $3,000 buys the two things every later step depends on: a clean drafting standard
that goes straight into your Revit templates, and a working tool that already takes the
notes / spec / schedule writing off your drafters' plates today.

## What you get for the $3,000

1. **Your 2D kit standardized.** Title blocks, block library, layer and annotation
   standards, documented in one place. This is not busywork for LT. It is the exact
   content that gets encoded into the Revit templates in Phase 1. Settle it once now, or
   re-argue it during Revit setup when it costs more.
2. **The a2o Draft Assistant, already built.** A private web app that writes General
   Notes, Spec Sections, and Schedules in a2o's voice, grounded on a2o's own past
   documents (not generic boilerplate). Edit in place, copy to any sheet, save and reuse
   by project. It is built, tested (67 automated tests), and running. Remaining work for
   Phase 0 is loading your real documents into it and tuning it to your conventions.
3. **Overflow drafting support** during the changeover, as needed.

## What Phase 0 is not (set expectations now)

- It does not build your Revit templates or family library. The proposal puts core Revit
  setup and training on a2o's track (in-house, a Revit specialist, or Autodesk onboarding).
  Keys2TheWeb can take that on as a separately quoted item if you want one vendor.
- It does not automate inside AutoCAD LT. LT cannot be extended. Phase 0 is relief
  around it, built so nothing is thrown away when Revit lands.

## Why do this before Revit instead of after

- The Draft Assistant's output is text. The same notes and schedules work on an LT sheet
  today and in a Revit template or schedule tomorrow. Nothing is wasted.
- Standardized drawings are the prerequisite for the one piece of automation that is
  cheap and certain: converting a2o's own DWGs into Revit models by layer and block
  name (weeks, not months). That only works if the drawings follow one standard.

## "Other AI tools work with Revit. How is this different?"

- Autodesk is shipping its own AI assistant inside Revit 2027 (model queries, sheets,
  schedules, automated documentation). Vendors like Civly sell sketch-to-model for
  roughly A$2,000 per seat per month, with code checks for Australian, US IBC, and EU
  codes, nothing for the Florida Building Code, flood and wind provisions, or HARC.
- Those tools are generic. They do not know a2o's note language, Keys jurisdictions,
  or HARC conventions. That is what Keys2TheWeb builds, and it is what the Draft
  Assistant already does.
- Position: buy the generic Revit AI once you are on Revit; Keys2TheWeb builds what is
  specific to a2o and makes the pieces work together. Phase 2 decides buy vs build
  from a real project, not a guess.

## What we need from a2o to start

1. Your current drafting standards and 2 to 3 typical past projects (DWG + PDF).
2. The go-ahead and the $3,000 invoice.

## What happens next

- Weeks 1 to 3: standardize the kit; load your documents into the Draft Assistant;
  hand it to the team with a short walkthrough.
- Phase 1 whenever you are ready: encode the standard into the Revit templates and
  coordinate the rollout ($1,500 to $2,500).
- Phase 2 after one real Revit project: document the gaps and quote any custom tool,
  including the DWG-to-Revit converter if the standard holds up ($2,500 assessment).
